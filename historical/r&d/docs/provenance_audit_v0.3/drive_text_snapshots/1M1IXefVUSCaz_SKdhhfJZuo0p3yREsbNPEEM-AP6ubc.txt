S.T.A.R. Claim/Evidence Registry
Version 0.1 — Initial Controlled Baseline
Governed by S.T.A.R. Research Charter v0.2
________________


1. Purpose
The S.T.A.R. Claim/Evidence Registry is the authoritative record of substantive mathematical, computational, empirical, theoretical, and speculative claims made within the S.T.A.R. research program.
The Registry exists to answer:
What does S.T.A.R. claim, what evidence supports the claim, what remains unverified, and what would falsify it?
The Registry is controlled by:
S.T.A.R. Research Charter v0.2
If a historical document conflicts with the epistemic standards of the Charter, the historical statement is preserved but its Registry classification is changed accordingly.
The Registry therefore separates:
[
\text{Historical Claim}
]
from
[
\text{Current Scientific Status}.
]
________________


2. Registry Governing Rules
Rule 1 — A claim is not evidence
The fact that a proposition appears in an S.T.A.R. document does not constitute evidence supporting that proposition.
________________


Rule 2 — A computation is not automatically an empirical validation
A computational result may establish:
* that code executed;
* that a mathematical quantity was calculated;
* that a numerical relationship occurred in a dataset;
* that a model fit a dataset.
It does not automatically establish:
* causation;
* physical reality;
* generalization;
* predictive power;
* mathematical theoremhood.
________________


Rule 3 — Historical claims are preserved
Older claims shall not be silently deleted.
Instead they receive one of the following statuses:
* Active;
* Supported;
* Preliminary;
* Exploratory;
* Unverified;
* Reclassified;
* Falsified;
* Superseded;
* Open.
________________


Rule 4 — The strongest version of a claim is not automatically retained
When a source contains both a cautious and an extremely strong formulation, the Registry records the actual historical formulation but evaluates it according to the Charter.
For example:
“Arithmetic structure correlates with cosmic topology”
and
“The universe is generated by arithmetic structure”
are separate claims.
Evidence for the first does not automatically support the second.
________________


Rule 5 — Model-dependent thresholds are not canonical
Historical thresholds such as:
[
W_2<10^{-2}
]
are recorded as exploratory/model-dependent unless independently calibrated.
________________


Rule 6 — Statistical significance and predictive utility are separate
A claim may have statistical significance without meaningful predictive utility.
Both must therefore be recorded separately.
________________


Rule 7 — Discovery and confirmation are separate
A model, mapping, equation, feature, threshold, or classifier discovered using a dataset cannot use the same dataset as its sole independent confirmation.
________________

4. Controlled Vocabulary
4.1 Epistemic Category
D — Derived
Mathematically follows from definitions and stated assumptions.
E — Empirical
Supported by reproducible observation or computation.
M — Model
A constructed mathematical or phenomenological model.
P — Postulate
An assumption deliberately adopted as a foundation of a proposed theory.
H — Hypothesis
A proposition being tested.
C — Conjecture
A proposition lacking proof or sufficient empirical confirmation.
T — Testable Prediction
A consequence capable of independent testing.
S — Speculative Interpretation
A possible interpretation not presently supported enough to function as an empirical hypothesis.
________________


5. Evidence Status Vocabulary
Status
	Meaning
	SUPPORTED
	Evidence currently meets Charter requirements for the stated claim
	PRELIMINARY
	Some evidence exists but important validation remains
	EXPLORATORY
	Useful observation/model, but not yet confirmatory
	UNVERIFIED
	Claim is stated but supporting evidence is insufficient
	RECLASSIFIED
	Historical claim has been downgraded under Charter v0.2
	FALSIFIED
	Specific formulation contradicted by documented evidence
	SUPERSEDED
	Replaced by a more precise formulation
	OPEN
	Scientifically unresolved
	ACTIVE TEST
	Currently undergoing experimental evaluation
	________________


6. Support Levels
S0 — Statement Only
Claim appears in a source but has no adequate supporting evidence.
S1 — Mathematical/Computational Construction
Object or computation exists.
S2 — Preliminary Correspondence
Initial empirical relationship exists.
S3 — Reproducible Statistical Correspondence
Result survives appropriate statistical and null testing.
S4 — Independent Replication
Result survives independent datasets or independent analysis.
S5 — Predictive Utility
Result demonstrates meaningful out-of-sample predictive performance.
S6 — Physical Theory
A mathematically consistent theory survives empirical physical constraints and produces novel successful predictions.
No claim may skip levels merely because its theoretical interpretation is ambitious.

19. Initial Baseline Rule
This Version 0.1 Registry establishes the following baseline:
Historical S.T.A.R. claims remain part of the research record, but their scientific status is determined by Research Charter v0.2 rather than by the language used in the original document.
This means that phrases such as:
* “proved”;
* “validated”;
* “theorem”;
* “law”;
* “definitive”;
* “confirmed”;
* “cosmos is”;
* “gravity is”;
* “dark matter is”;
must be interpreted according to their Registry classification.
A historical statement may remain quoted as historical evidence of what the project previously proposed without being treated as a current scientific conclusion.

22. Registry Closing Statement
The Claim/Evidence Registry is not intended to determine in advance whether ACSC, GLMPCT, ECC, RTCH, or the broader S.T.A.R. framework is correct.
Its purpose is more fundamental:
To ensure that every claim has a known epistemic status, every piece of evidence has a defined role, every prediction has a falsification condition, and every theoretical interpretation remains distinguishable from the observations that motivate it.
Accordingly:
[
\boxed{
\textbf{S.T.A.R. preserves every claim.}
}
]
[
\boxed{
\textbf{S.T.A.R. tests every testable claim.}
}
]
[
\boxed{
\textbf{S.T.A.R. distinguishes models from evidence.}
}
]
[
\boxed{
\textbf{S.T.A.R. allows negative results to remain scientific results.}
}
]
[
\boxed{
\textbf{The evidence determines the status of the theory.}
}
]
