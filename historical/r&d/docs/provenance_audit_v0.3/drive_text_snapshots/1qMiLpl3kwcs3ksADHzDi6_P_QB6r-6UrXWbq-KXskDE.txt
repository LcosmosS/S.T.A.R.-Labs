The law relates the star formation rate surface density (Σ_SFR) to the gas surface density (Σ_gas) by a power law (typically, Σ_SFR ∝ Σ_gas^n, with n ≈ 1.4).
Here are some ways to incorporate this physical model:
1. Create a Predicted SFR Feature:
 If you have a proxy or measurement for gas surface density (or a related quantity like CO or HI flux), you can compute an expected SFR:

   * For example, using a normalized constant A, compute:
expected_SFR = A * (gas_density)^1.4

   * This feature can be added as an input so the model learns deviations from the physical law.

      2. Residual Feature:
 If your dataset includes an observed SFR and you compute an expected SFR from the Kennicutt–Schmidt law, you can create a residual feature:

         * residual_SFR = observed_SFR - expected_SFR
 This may capture additional physics or environmental effects not explained by the simple law.

            3. Interaction Terms:
 You could include interaction terms between gas-related proxies and other features (like metallicity or stellar mass) that might affect the star formation efficiency. For example, if you have gas density and stellar mass, you might compute an interaction like:

               * gas_density × stellar_mass

 The Kennicutt–Schmidt law is often expressed in surface densities. Ensure that your gas density proxy is appropriately normalized (e.g., in units of M☉/pc²) before applying the power law.

    max_depth = trial.suggest_int("max_depth", 5, 30)
