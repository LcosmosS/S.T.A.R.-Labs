### 1. The Coquereaux Correspondence: Mathematical Foundations


The strategic evolution of cosmological modeling requires moving beyond purely phenomenological descriptions toward frameworks with clearer mathematical grounding. At the heart of the S.T.A.R. (Symbolic–Topological–Arithmetic Relativity) initiative lies the Coquereaux correspondence (Coquereaux 2014/2015). When the Friedmann–Lemaître equations—including contributions from matter, radiation, spatial curvature, and a cosmological constant—are expressed in conformal time rather than cosmic time, their solutions (the scale factor, densities, temperature, and Hubble function) can be written in terms of Weierstrass elliptic functions. Consequently, any concrete cosmic history specified by present-day density parameters corresponds to a lattice \(\mathcal{L}\) in the complex plane and therefore to an elliptic curve possessing definite Weierstrass invariants \((g_2, g_3)\), modular parameter \(\tau\), and \(j\)-invariant.


This establishes an exact link between classical cosmology and the theory of elliptic curves. The \(j\)-invariant classifies isomorphism classes of the underlying curve, while the modular parameter \(\tau\) encodes the ratio of the lattice periods.


The Arithmetic–Cosmic Structure Conjecture (ACSC) takes this correspondence as its starting point and explores a further, still phenomenological step: whether the *arithmetic* geometry of elliptic curves—particularly the discrete invariants of curves defined over \(\mathbb{Q}\) (rank, conductor, real period \(\Omega_E\), regulator, discriminant, etc.)—can supply a natural discrete skeleton or weighting for continuous cosmic geometry and expansion history. In this sense ACSC is an arithmetic ansatz inspired by, but not logically entailed by, the Coquereaux correspondence.


Within the Weierstrass model \(y^2 = x^3 + ax + b\) we adopt the working identifications \(a \approx -\kappa r\) (global structuring force associated with comoving distance) and \(b = \rho\) (local compressive force associated with scaled density). These assignments, together with the numerical value of \(\kappa\), were obtained by constrained symbolic regression under the ACSC projection; they are not first-principles derivations and remain subject to refinement or replacement by better-motivated forms.

### 2. The S.T.A.R. Model: Architecture of Arithmetic-Cosmic Structure


The S.T.A.R. Model is a unified computational pipeline that treats the universe as a symbolic manifold whose large-scale features may be informed by arithmetic data. It operates through three interacting layers:


- **ACSC (Arithmetic Projection Geometry).** Defines a projection map \(\Phi: \mathcal{E} \to M_{\rm cosmo}\) that sends selected invariants of elliptic curves (drawn from databases such as Cremona and LMFDB) to coordinates on a three-dimensional symbolic manifold. Real periods \(\Omega_E\) and regulators \(\operatorname{Reg}(E)\) are among the quantities encoded as geometric data.
- **ECC (Entropy Cohomology).** Information flow is described by an entropy field \(\mathcal{M}\) and its associated curvature. Entropy curvature functions as a symbolic analogue of Ricci curvature and modifies effective expansion along cohomology flow lines.
- **TDA & Symbolic Regression.** Topological data analysis (persistent homology, Wasserstein distances between persistence diagrams, Betti numbers) is used to compare the arithmetic point cloud with observed cosmic structure. Symbolic regression discovers candidate functional relations between arithmetic and cosmological quantities under explicit complexity constraints.


**Metric perturbation mechanism.** In contrast to standard general relativity, where metric perturbations are sourced by mass-density fluctuations, the S.T.A.R. metric perturbation \(\delta g\) is constructed from the distribution of projected arithmetic invariants. The perturbation is scale-dependent. A Natural Normalization constant \(K \approx 1.003\), measured on a 978-galaxy sample under the current projection, is observed to remain close to unity; this proximity is interpreted as a diagnostic of stability across the sampled range of scales. These components together furnish the variables required by Symbolic-Field Theory.

### 4. The Hubble–Planck Tension as Scale-Dependent Geometry


Within the S.T.A.R. framework the Hubble tension is interpreted as a possible signature of scale-dependent geometry. Local distance-ladder determinations (\(H_0\approx 73\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\)) preferentially sample regions influenced by positive symbolic curvature, while high-redshift CMB inferences (\(H_0\approx 67.4\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\)) average over a more global distribution.


A working parametrization of the effective expansion history takes the form  
\[
H_{\rm eff}^2(z) = H_0^2\Bigl[\Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_\Lambda + az + bz^2\Bigr],
\]
where the correction terms \(az + bz^2\) arise from the coupling between the arithmetic projection and entropy flux. At low redshift (\(z\lesssim 0.5\)) these terms can enhance the apparent expansion rate. The numerical coefficients and the overall normalization are constrained by the same regression and normalization procedures described above; their stability is monitored by the observed proximity of \(K\) to unity. Whether this mechanism survives stronger null tests and independent data remains an open question.
