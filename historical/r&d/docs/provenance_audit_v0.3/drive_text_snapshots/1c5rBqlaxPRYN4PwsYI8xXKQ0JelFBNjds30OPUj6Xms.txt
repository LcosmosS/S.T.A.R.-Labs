S.T.A.R. Experiment Registry
Version 0.2
Operational Specification for Arithmetic–Geometric–Topological Investigation of Cosmic Structure
________________


1. Purpose
The S.T.A.R. Experiment Registry defines the controlled experimental program required to evaluate the hypotheses, mathematical constructions, phenomenological mappings, and theoretical extensions identified in the S.T.A.R. Research Charter and Claim/Evidence Registry.
The registry converts research claims into reproducible experiments with explicit:
* experiment identifiers;
* associated claims;
* scientific questions;
* datasets;
* data provenance requirements;
* independent and dependent variables;
* mathematical constructions;
* mapping choices;
* locked parameters;
* null models;
* train/test or calibration/validation design;
* statistical tests;
* success and failure conditions;
* reproducibility requirements;
* historical evidence status;
* reconstruction requirements;
* replication requirements.
The registry is intended to prevent the project from moving directly from a mathematical construction to an interpretation of physical reality without an intermediate experimental chain.
The governing principle is:
Do not ask whether the theory can be made to fit the universe. Ask whether a fixed mathematical construction predicts structure that was not used to construct it.
________________

3. Epistemic Status
Every experiment must distinguish the status of the claim being tested.
D — Derived
Mathematically derived from stated assumptions.
E — Empirical
Observed in an astronomical or simulated dataset.
M — Model
A phenomenological or computational construction whose physical interpretation remains open.
P — Postulate
An explicitly assumed component of the framework.
H — Hypothesis
A proposition being tested.
C — Conjecture
A broader mathematical or physical proposition not yet established.
T — Testable Prediction
A quantitative prediction whose outcome can discriminate between hypotheses or null models.
S — Speculative
A possible interpretation or future extension that is not currently experimentally established.
An experiment may test multiple epistemic levels, but the registry must not collapse them into a single status.
________________


4. Evidence-State Classification
Each experiment receives an evidence-state designation.
U — Usable Historical R&D
Existing R&D contains sufficiently specific information to support direct use or limited reconstruction.
R — Reconstruction Required
Historical work exists, but preprocessing, leakage control, parameter locking, provenance, or statistical design must be reconstructed before the result can be treated as controlled evidence.
N — New Experiment Required
Existing R&D does not constitute a controlled experiment sufficient for the claim.
C — Control
The experiment exists primarily to establish a baseline, null, or alternative explanation.
D — Derivation Audit
The primary task is mathematical derivation or consistency checking.
H — Historical Only
The material is preserved for research lineage but is not treated as current evidence.
X — Negative/Diagnostic
The result is useful because it identifies instability, failure, sensitivity, or a limitation.

7. EXP-CTRL-A03 — Leakage and Contamination Audit
Priority: A
Evidence State: R
Associated Claims: CORE-001, SFR-A001, COSMO-L001
Objective
Determine whether historical S.T.A.R. machine-learning and feature-engineering experiments contain information leakage between training and testing populations.
Historical motivation
Several historical scripts construct:
* normalized variables;
* redshift bins;
* (L_{\rm cosmo}(s));
* cosmo_rank;
* interaction terms;
before the train/test split.
Therefore historical performance values cannot automatically be interpreted as leakage-free predictive measurements.
Procedure
Reconstruct the relevant pipelines so that:
[
D_{\rm train}
\rightarrow
\text{fit preprocessing}
\rightarrow
D_{\rm train}^{\prime},
]
and the fitted transformations are subsequently applied to:
[
D_{\rm test}
\rightarrow
D_{\rm test}^{\prime}.
]
No target-derived or population-derived quantity may be calculated using the complete dataset before partitioning.
Required controls
Compare:
1. historical pipeline;
2. leakage-controlled pipeline;
3. shuffled-target pipeline.
Success Criterion
The reconstructed pipeline must reproduce the historical result within a documented tolerance where no leakage is present.
Failure Criterion
Any material performance degradation attributable to information leakage must be recorded and the historical result reclassified accordingly.

35. Null-Model Standards
Every core claim must have an explicit null.
Possible null families include:
N1 — Label permutation
Break correspondence while preserving marginals.
N2 — Spatial permutation
Preserve arithmetic distributions while destroying spatial association.
N3 — Matched random control
Generate a random control with matched sample size and distribution.
N4 — Alternative invariant
Replace rank with another arithmetic invariant.
N5 — Alternative mapping
Replace the proposed mapping with a controlled alternative.
N6 — Complexity-matched sequence
Replace Fibonacci/Lucas with sequences of comparable complexity.
N7 — Astrophysical baseline
Determine whether ordinary astrophysical variables already explain the effect.

the input invariants, normalization, free parameters, parameter-search range, and transformation.
The mapping must be fixed before final validation.
A mapping that is selected after observing the validation result is not considered independent validation.
________________


37. Parameter Locking
Each experiment must distinguish:
Locked parameters
Parameters specified before final evaluation.
Training parameters
Parameters estimated from the training data.
Search parameters
Parameters explored during model selection.
Historical parameters
Parameters inherited from previous R&D but not yet validated.
Historical values such as:
[
\lambda_r=200
]
must be explicitly marked historical until independently tested.
________________


38. Reproducibility Requirements
A completed Priority A experiment must preserve:
1. source dataset identifier;
2. dataset checksum/version;
3. preprocessing code;
4. experiment code;
5. parameter file;
6. random seeds;
7. train/test split;
8. null-generation procedure;
9. output metrics;
10. plots/tables;
11. environment/package information;
12. experiment manifest.
A result that cannot be reconstructed from these artifacts remains historical evidence.
________________


39. Results Classification
Experimental results should ultimately be classified as:
R0 — Failed/Unreproducible
Cannot reproduce the historical result.
R1 — Reproduced Historically
Historical result reproduced but not independently validated.
R2 — Controlled
Result survives leakage and null controls.
R3 — Replicated
Result survives an independent dataset.
R4 — Predictive
Result provides meaningful out-of-sample predictive performance.
R5 — Physical
Result is connected to a mathematically consistent physical theory and independently tested physical prediction.
These levels correspond to the broader S.T.A.R. success ladder.

41. Historical R&D Integration
The existing R&D archive should not be discarded.
It becomes the historical experimental reservoir.
Historical scripts, datasets, figures, numerical results, failed models, alternative mappings, and exploratory analyses should be linked to experiment IDs.
The archive therefore answers:
“What has already been attempted?”
The Experiment Registry answers:
“What experiment do we now consider controlled?”
The Results Registry will answer:
“What did the controlled experiment actually show?”
The Replication Registry will answer:
“Did it survive independent validation?”

43. Registry Governance
No experiment may be marked Complete merely because a script ran successfully.
Completion requires:
[
\text{Specification}
+
\text{Data Provenance}
+
\text{Locked Parameters}
+
\text{Null Model}
+
\text{Execution}
+
\text{Result}
+
\text{Reproducibility}.
]
No historical result may be silently upgraded to evidence.
No statistically significant result may be silently upgraded to predictive usefulness.
No predictive result may be silently upgraded to physical theory.
No physical interpretation may be treated as established without independent validation.
