1. Executive Premise: The Earth‑Map Paradox as the Foundational Thought Experiment
This proposal originates from a terrestrial paradox: the challenge of representing an oblate spheroid on a flat plane without inheriting curvature‑induced distortions. Traditional cartographic projections expose this tension. The Mercator projection preserves angles for navigation but introduces extreme area distortions—rendering Greenland (2.2 million km²) comparable to Africa (30.4 million km²). Equal‑area projections such as Gall‑Peters preserve area but distort shape. The AuthaGraph projection balances distortions but requires discrete tiling.
The core insight motivating this work is that local curvature can be suppressed through massive radial scaling, producing a “patchwork quilt” of true areas. By scaling the radius of curvature to an asymptotic limit, the surface becomes locally Euclidean while remaining globally spherical. This reframes the cartographic paradox as a topological affordance: local flatness and global curvature can coexist when curvature is treated as a scalable parameter.
1. This thought experiment forms the philosophical seed of the ACSC:
