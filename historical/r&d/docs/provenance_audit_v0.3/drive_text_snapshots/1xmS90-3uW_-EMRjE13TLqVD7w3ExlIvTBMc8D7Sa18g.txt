From Numerical Anchor to Geometric Principle: Justifying the Initial Mapping


Our first step in bridging the cosmos to the curve is guided by numerical inspiration, not a pre-existing physical formula. To anchor the abstract analogy in the tangible universe, we use known cosmological values as a starting point. The comoving distance to the Virgo Cluster, 54 million light-years, directly inspires the choice of a coefficient related to distance. 
This initial mapping is a deliberate act of grounding—an attempt to see if the numbers of the universe will resonate within the arithmetic framework. It is not based on a physics equation that demands r become a or ρ become b, but on the principle of starting with a known cosmic truth.
However, for this framework to evolve from a compelling coincidence into a robust theory, this initial choice requires a deeper, speculative justification.
Why should an expansive parameter like distance (r) correspond to the a coefficient, while a compressive one like density (ρ) maps to b? 
The theoretical rationale lies not in a physical law we have discovered, but in the intrinsic geometric roles these coefficients play in defining the fundamental properties of the elliptic curve itself, y2=x3+ax+b.


* The a Coefficient as a Global Structuring Force: The coefficient a is mapped from the Comoving Distance (r). Its mathematical role is to influence the global shape of the cubic polynomial +ax+b, specifically by controlling the location of its extrema. This makes it a natural analogue for expansive cosmological parameters. In our model, a relates to the global geometrical scale and the curvature gradient. This is consistent with the proposed projection function, Φ(E), which uses logarithmic embeddings of the curve's invariants to define the angular coordinates (ϕ,θ) on a resulting spherical projection, analogous to mapping stars onto a celestial sphere.


   * The b Coefficient as a Local Compressive Force: The coefficient b is mapped from the Scaled Density (ρ). Its mathematical role is to shift the curve vertically, which strongly influences the discriminant, Δ=−16(4a3+27b2), and the position of the curve's roots. This makes it a fitting analogue for compressive parameters that control local, dense topological features. The emergent cosmology of our framework suggests that "Elevation approximates symbolic mass" in the final topological map. Since density (ρ) is explicitly linked to topography in the proposed 3D map and is used to define the cosmic L-function, Lcosmo​(s), through density ratios, its connection to b is geometrically sound.


This rationale crystallizes the guiding principle behind our mapping: the choice of a and b is not arbitrary but is a hypothesis rooted in geometric function. The a coefficient controls the global structure and spatial framework (r), while the b coefficient governs local matter concentration and topography (ρ). This provides a theoretical foundation for our initial numerical intuition, transforming an inspired guess into a testable geometric principle.
