2.0 Preliminaries: Defining the Arithmetic-Cosmological Lexicon
Before a theorem connecting two disparate fields can be stated, its terms must be unambiguously defined. 2.1 The Cosmological Domain: Observational Quantities
* Comoving Volume: This is the volume of a region of space measured relative to the expanding fabric of spacetime, ensuring it remains constant as the universe expands.
* Virial Imbalance (|2T + U|): This is a measure of a system's deviation from virial equilibrium, derived from its total kinetic (T) and potential (U) energies.
* Scaled Density (ρ): This is a representation of a cosmic structure's density, benchmarked against a known reference value, such as that of the Virgo Cluster.
* Physical Observables: These are fundamental inputs from astronomical surveys like the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), including a galaxy's stellar mass, its redshift (z), and its comoving distance (r).
2.2 The Arithmetic Domain: Elliptic Curve Invariants
* Algebraic Rank (r): This is the number of independent rational points of infinite order on an elliptic curve over the rational numbers (ℚ).
* Regulator (R or Reg(E)): This is a measure of the "volume" of the lattice formed by the rational points on the curve.
* Real Period (Ω): This is an invariant related to the geometric size of the curve, analogous to the period of a trigonometric function.
* Torsion Subgroup (T): This is the group of rational points on the curve that have finite order.
* Discriminant (Δ): This is a quantity derived from the curve's coefficients that determines if the curve is non-singular and encodes its fundamental complexity.
* Conductor (N): This is an integer that measures the arithmetic complexity of the curve, specifically encoding the prime numbers where the curve has "bad reduction."
