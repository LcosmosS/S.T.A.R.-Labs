Step 1: Recap the BSD Conjecture
The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture is a fundamental problem in number theory, specifically for elliptic curves over \mathbb{Q}. It has two parts:


* Weak BSD: The rank of the Mordell-Weil group E(\mathbb{Q}) (the algebraic rank) equals the order of the zero of the L-function ( L(E, s) ) at s = 1 (the analytic rank).
* Strong BSD: The leading coefficient of the Taylor expansion of ( L(E, s) ) at s = 1 is given by:
\lim_{s \to 1} \frac{L(E, s)}{(s-1)^r} = \frac{\Omega \cdot \text{Reg}(E) \cdot |\text{Sha}(E)| \cdot \prod_p c_p}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2}


Where:


* ( r ) is the rank of E(\mathbb{Q}).
* \Omega is the real period.
* \text{Reg}(E) is the regulator.
* |\text{Sha}(E)| is the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group.
* \prod_p c_p is the product of Tamagawa numbers.
* |E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}| is the order of the torsion subgroup.
Proving the BSD conjecture requires showing that this holds for all elliptic curves over \mathbb{Q}, not just a single curve.

Step 1: Understand Euler Systems and Their Role in BSD
An Euler system is a collection of cohomology classes associated with an elliptic curve ( E ) over \mathbb{Q}, satisfying certain compatibility conditions across number fields. These classes are used to bound the Selmer group, which in turn bounds the rank of E(\mathbb{Q}) and the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group \text{Sha}(E). The BSD conjecture states:


* Weak BSD: The rank of E(\mathbb{Q}) (algebraic rank) equals the order of the zero of ( L(E, s) ) at s = 1 (analytic rank).
* Strong BSD:
\lim_{s \to 1} \frac{L(E, s)}{(s-1)^r} = \frac{\Omega \cdot \text{Reg}(E) \cdot |\text{Sha}(E)| \cdot \prod_p c_p}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2}


Euler systems help by:


1. Constructing rational points on ( E ), relating the algebraic rank to the analytic rank.
2. Bounding \text{Sha}(E), which is crucial for the strong BSD formula.
Historical Context


* Kolyvagin (1989): Used an Euler system of Heegner points to prove weak BSD for elliptic curves with analytic rank \leq 1. He showed that if L(E, 1) \neq 0, the rank is 0, and if L'(E, 1) \neq 0, the rank is 1, with \text{Sha}(E) finite.
* Kato (2004): Developed a p-adic Euler system to bound the p-part of \text{Sha}(E), proving parts of BSD for many curves.
* Modern Developments: Higher-rank Euler systems (e.g., Jetchev, Skinner-Urban) aim to extend these results to all ranks.
