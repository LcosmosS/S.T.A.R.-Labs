Introduction to the Arithmetic-Cosmic Structure Conjecture (ACSC)

The cosmos, as it unfolds before us in all its measurable depth and symbolic intricacy, presents not merely a physical frontier but an arithmetic structure waiting to be mapped. The Arithmetic–Cosmic Structure Conjecture (ACSC) posits that the distribution and evolution of cosmic matter can be interpreted through analogues of number-theoretic systems—most notably, elliptic curves, L-functions, and the conjectural framework of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) Conjecture.

In this introduction, we aim to lay the groundwork for the ACSC by motivating the central question:

Can the structure and behavior of large-scale cosmic entities—galaxies, voids, and filaments—be rigorously described using the language of arithmetic geometry and algebraic number theory?

This inquiry is not rhetorical; it emerges from a careful empirical analysis of galactic distributions, stellar masses, and star formation rates (SFR) across a cosmological volume, interpreted through symbolic functions derived from elliptic curves and generalized Dirichlet series.

Motivation

Traditional cosmological models employ general relativity, hydrodynamics, and statistical mechanics to describe the universe at various scales. However, certain global behaviors—especially those involving hierarchical structure formation, persistent homology, and scaling symmetries—invite us to consider whether these macroscopic laws encode an underlying arithmetic logic. The motivation for ACSC stems from this very tension: Are the macroscopic laws emergent from an invisible symbolic geometry?

The ACSC suggests that each galaxy can be mapped as a rational point on a cosmic analogue of an elliptic curve, where their properties (e.g., mass, metallicity, SFR) correspond to invariants such as discriminant, conductor, and rank. The function that mediates this mapping, denoted Φ: E → M_cosmo, becomes central to the projection logic of the conjecture.

Moreover, just as the BSD conjecture relates the algebraic structure of elliptic curves to their analytic properties, the ACSC seeks to draw a direct correspondence between the measurable distributions of galaxies and the symbolic structure of L-functions. The bridge is not merely metaphorical—it is computational, observational, and theoretical. This conceptual framework allows us to treat cosmological data as a trace over an arithmetic field, wherein the entropy and energy distributions within cosmic structure manifest as terms in a symbolic series. This approach pushes beyond physical modeling into the realm of symbolic arithmetic geometry.

Framing the Problem

Our key assumption is that large-scale cosmic configurations are not random but encode information that can be decoded via symbolic projection:

- Each galaxy has a set of observable quantities: log M_gas, Z, A_V, SFR, v_sigma, ...
- Each of these can be embedded as terms in a symbolic entropy function or L-function analogue: L(cosmos, s)
- The goal is to estimate whether this function admits critical values analogous to those in the BSD conjecture, and whether its behavior aligns with observable topology.

The structure of this mapping introduces deeper questions: Can we construct a functional equation for L(cosmos, s)? Does the function admit an analytic continuation akin to traditional Dirichlet series? Can cosmic voids and topological curvature be seen as analogues of zeroes in L-functions, indicating structural transitions or phase boundaries in symbolic space?

If affirmative, these would provide the scaffolding for a true arithmetic-cosmic dictionary—where galaxies, voids, and filaments are not only structures of matter but bearers of symbolic meaning. Each observable becomes a coordinate in a new projection space: one that is determined not by position alone, but by arithmetic relations.

This chapter serves as the starting point to formalize these ideas, leading toward a symbolic geometric re-interpretation of cosmic structure, tested and refined through Python- and PARI/GP-based modeling and data from SDSS, GAMA, and MaNGA surveys.

Projection Functions and Elliptic Curve Analogues in Cosmological Structure
Defining the Projection Map Φ

At the heart of the ACSC lies a symbolic projection map Φ, designed to bridge the abstract structure of elliptic curves 𝔈 with the empirical manifold of cosmic measurements ℳ_cosmo.

This projection is not spatial, but structural and symbolic. The elliptic curve 𝔈 in this context refers to an abstract arithmetic object, typically defined over ℚ, with rational points encoding deep number-theoretic properties. On the cosmological side, ℳ_cosmo represents a high-dimensional space whose axes are observational quantities: stellar mass, metallicity, gas content, star formation rate, dust attenuation, and kinematic properties.

We define Φ as:

   Φ: Pᵢ ∈ 𝔈(ℚ) ↦ Gᵢ ∈ ℳ_cosmo

where Pᵢ denotes a rational point on the elliptic curve, and Gᵢ is the corresponding galaxy. The guiding hypothesis is that there exists a structural similarity—whether through symbolic entropy, L-values, or rank analogues—that validates this projection.

In this section, we explore the construction of Φ, its assumptions, and its necessary properties:

- It must be injective on equivalence classes of curves.
- It must preserve symbolic invariants (e.g., discriminant, conductor ↔ mass and metallicity).
- It must enable the construction of a symbolic L-function whose convergence and behavior reflect empirical measurements.
