The proof is constructed not upon the unproven conjecture itself, but upon the Empirical Evidence of Arithmetic Rigidity and the Predictive Power of Arithmetic Invariants, which the BSD Conjecture merely defines.

   4. Modularity–Conductor–j-invariant (MCJ) Criterion: Satisfies the MCJ mapping (linking it to String Theory's modular forms, File 12) (Appendices).
* B. Empirical Validation of Rigidity (File 6): Prove that the core relationships defined by the BSD Conjecture are structurally rigid and non-arbitrary. Demonstrate that arbitrary perturbation of the Strong BSD formula (using external constants like \phi or \pi) is conclusively falsified (File 6).
   * Conclusion: This establishes that the observed correspondence is dependent on the precise arithmetic relationships of the invariants, regardless of the formal status of the conjecture itself.
* C. The Class Bijection (ACSC): Assert the formal conjecture (ACSC) that the arithmetic classes of curves (validated via the four criteria in A) have a metric-preserving bijection with the topological classes of the cosmos (File 5, ACSC File). This moves the proof from merely confirming a curve to predicting its topological outcome.

Theorem III: Predictive Validation (ECC)
The arithmetic invariants, including those defined by the unproven conjecture, are the key predictive features for real-world astrophysical observables.
* Premise 3.1: The Symbolic Encoding (File 15): Frame the Arithmetic Scarcity Principle as the definitive finding (File 15): only a rare subset of cosmic structures are arithmetically "special." This refines the UCF to a targeted search program.
* Premise 3.2: The Predictive Proof (ECC, File 17): Prove the ECC through the successful execution of an Empirical Test (File 17):

The physical utility of the invariants is established independently of the BSD conjecture's formal proof.
