Scientific Claim, Evidence, Provenance, and Validation Control System

1. Purpose
The S.T.A.R. Claim/Evidence Registry records every substantive mathematical, physical, astronomical, computational, statistical, and methodological claim made within the S.T.A.R. research program.
The registry exists to prevent exploratory results, historical interpretations, mathematical derivations, computational observations, and empirically validated results from being treated as equivalent forms of evidence.
The registry therefore separates:
1. what is mathematically derived;
2. what has been computationally reproduced;
3. what has been empirically observed;
4. what is a model or phenomenological construction;
5. what is hypothesized;
6. what is conjectured;
7. what constitutes a testable prediction;
8. what remains speculative;
9. what has failed, become unstable, or remains unresolved.
The registry is subordinate to the S.T.A.R. Research Charter and is the authoritative index connecting claims to:
[
\boxed{
\text{Claim}
\rightarrow
\text{Evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{Data}
\rightarrow
\text{Code}
\rightarrow
\text{Experiment}
\rightarrow
\text{Result}
\rightarrow
\text{Replication}
}
]
No historical result becomes established evidence merely because it appears in an earlier S.T.A.R. document.
________________


2. Governing Scientific Principle
The registry adopts the following governing principle:
Do not ask whether the theory can be made to fit the universe. Ask whether a fixed mathematical construction predicts structure that was not used to construct it.
Consequently, a claim gains evidentiary status only through a traceable chain of derivation, computation, controlled experiment, independent validation, or replication appropriate to the type of claim.
________________


3. Epistemic Classification System
Every claim shall receive at least one primary epistemic classification.
D — Mathematically Derived
A result follows from explicitly stated mathematical assumptions, definitions, axioms, or previously established results.
Examples:
* algebraic identities;
* proven convergence under stated assumptions;
* Euler–Lagrange equations derived from an explicitly defined action;
* differential/geometric identities.
A D classification does not imply that the assumptions describe nature.
________________


E — Empirically Observed
A property measured or directly calculated from an astronomical or experimental dataset.
Examples:
* an observed correlation;
* a measured distribution;
* a reproducible dataset statistic;
* an observed residual pattern.
Evidence must identify the population, dataset, processing procedure, and uncertainty.
________________


M — Model / Phenomenological Construction
A mathematical or computational construction introduced to represent a physical or astronomical system but not yet established as fundamental.
Examples:
[
L_{\rm cosmo}(s)
]
or a proposed arithmetic-to-geometric mapping.
M status is not a claim that the construction is physically correct.
________________


P — Postulate
A foundational assumption deliberately introduced by the theory.
Postulates are not empirical evidence.
________________


H — Hypothesis
A proposed relationship whose truth remains to be determined experimentally or mathematically.
Examples:
Arithmetic rank may correspond to cosmic-web complexity.
________________


C — Conjecture
A stronger mathematical or theoretical proposition proposed for future proof or falsification.
________________


T — Testable Prediction
A claim that produces a measurable consequence that can distinguish the proposed model from an appropriate null or competing model.
A T classification should identify:
* observable;
* dataset;
* expected relation;
* null;
* acceptance/failure criterion.
________________


S — Speculative Interpretation
An interpretation extending beyond presently demonstrated mathematics or empirical evidence.
S claims must not be presented as established conclusions.
________________


4. Evidence Provenance Classification
In addition to epistemic classification, every evidentiary item receives a provenance classification.
THEORETICAL
Derived from mathematical reasoning.
SIMULATION
Produced through controlled synthetic data or numerical simulation.
PILOT OBSERVATION
Observed in real data but not yet sufficiently controlled or independently replicated.
HISTORICAL MODEL
Generated by an earlier S.T.A.R. model, script, or analysis.
CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT
Produced by a registered experiment with locked methodology and appropriate controls.
INDEPENDENT REPLICATION
Repeated independently using data, code, or methodology not used to establish the original result.
EXPLORATORY
Useful for discovery but not sufficient to establish evidence.
NEGATIVE / NULL
A null, failed prediction, instability, or result inconsistent with the proposed relationship.
UNVERIFIED
A result or statement for which the supporting computation, derivation, or evidence has not yet been independently checked.
________________

26. Machine-Learning Evidence Policy
Machine learning may be used for:
1. feature discovery;
2. nonlinear association detection;
3. predictive benchmarking;
4. residual analysis;
5. symbolic-regression hypothesis generation.
Machine learning shall not be treated as mathematical proof.
The required pipeline is:
[
\boxed{
\text{ML discovery}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate equation}
\rightarrow
\text{independent validation}
\rightarrow
\text{analytic derivation}
}
]
A high (R^2), low loss, SHAP importance, or symbolic-regression expression does not by itself establish a physical law.
________________


27. Leakage and Contamination Policy
Historical S.T.A.R. scripts contain cases where derived quantities, normalization, redshift binning, feature construction, or related transformations may have been calculated before the train/test split.
These results shall therefore receive:
Leakage Status: POTENTIAL LEAKAGE
until reconstructed.
A historical model shall not be promoted to controlled evidence until:
\varnothing
]
at the appropriate object/field level and all learned preprocessing parameters are derived exclusively from the training data.
________________


28. Statistical Evidence Policy
Statistical significance is not equivalent to practical predictive usefulness.
The registry therefore distinguishes:
Level 1 — Statistical Correspondence
A statistically significant relationship is detected.
Level 2 — Cross-Dataset Replication
The relationship survives independent datasets.
Level 3 — Predictive Correspondence
The relationship produces useful out-of-sample predictions.
Level 4 — Physical Theory
A mathematically coherent physical mechanism reproduces established limits and makes novel successful predictions.
Thus:
[
\text{significance}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{predictive utility}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{physical theory}.
]
________________


29. Null Model Requirements
Every empirical claim must specify an appropriate null.
Candidate null families include:
N1 — Permutation Null
Arithmetic assignments randomly permuted relative to astronomical objects.
N2 — Spatial Randomization
Spatial coordinates randomized while preserving relevant marginal distributions.
N3 — Invariant Replacement
Rank replaced by another arithmetic invariant.
N4 — Mapping Replacement
Candidate mapping replaced by a control mapping.
N5 — Synthetic Distribution Null
Data generated from known distributions without the proposed relationship.
N6 — Astrophysical Baseline
Conventional astrophysical variables used without arithmetic variables.
N7 — Random Feature Control
Randomly selected features used to establish model-capacity baselines.

41. Final Registry Principle
The Claim/Evidence Registry is not intended to prove the S.T.A.R. framework.

\boxed{
\text{Claim}
\neq
\text{Evidence}
\neq
\text{Experiment}
\neq
\text{Result}

\text{Theory}
