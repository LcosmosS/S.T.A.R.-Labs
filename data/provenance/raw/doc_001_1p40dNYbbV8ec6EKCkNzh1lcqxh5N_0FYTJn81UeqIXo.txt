S.T.A.R. Research Charter
Scientific Specification for Arithmetic–Geometric–Topological Investigation of Cosmic Structure
Project: S.T.A.R. Program
Status: Research Charter v0.2 — Foundational Specification
Purpose: Canonical scientific specification for the S.T.A.R. research program
Primary domains: Arithmetic geometry, topology, topological data analysis, astrophysics, cosmology, statistical inference, mathematical physics, machine learning
Controlling Scientific Standard for the Arithmetic–Geometric–Topological Investigation of Cosmic Structure
________________


1. Purpose
The S.T.A.R. program investigates whether mathematical structures arising from arithmetic geometry can be mapped into geometric and topological structures in a manner that produces statistically nontrivial, reproducible, and independently testable correspondence with astronomical observations.
The central research question is:
Can arithmetic invariants be mapped into geometric and topological structures in a way that produces statistically nontrivial, reproducible correspondence with independent astronomical data?
The program is therefore an investigation of a possible correspondence between:
[
\text{Arithmetic}
\rightarrow
\text{Geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{Topology}
\rightarrow
\text{Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{Physics}.
]
The project does not assume that elliptic curves determine the universe.
In particular, S.T.A.R. does not adopt any of the following as established facts:
* that elliptic curves determine cosmic structure;
* that elliptic-curve rank is equivalent to physical time;
* that elliptic-curve rank is equivalent to gravity;
* that arithmetic structure replaces the Einstein field equations;
* that dark matter is arithmetic curvature;
* that dark energy is rank growth;
* that cosmic topology is identical to arithmetic topology;
* that a unique arithmetic-to-cosmic mapping exists;
* that a strict bijection between arithmetic objects and astronomical objects exists;
* that the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture is required for the research program;
* that historical numerical correlations establish a physical law.
These remain hypotheses, candidate models, conjectures, or speculative interpretations unless and until they satisfy the evidentiary standards defined by this Charter.
The Charter is the controlling standard for evaluating all subsequent S.T.A.R. claims, experiments, mappings, computational results, theoretical constructions, and proposed physical interpretations.
________________


2. Scientific Scope
S.T.A.R. consists of several connected but epistemically distinct research layers.
Layer I — Arithmetic
The program studies mathematical objects and invariants including elliptic curves, rank, conductor, discriminant, regulator, torsion, periods, Frobenius traces, and related quantities.
Layer II — Arithmetic Geometry
Arithmetic invariants are transformed into candidate geometric representations through mappings such as:
(\phi_E,\theta_E,z_E,s_E).
]
A simplified candidate representation is:
\left(
\log|\Delta_E|,
\log N_E,
\alpha r_E
\right).
]
Layer III — Arithmetic Cartography
Mapped arithmetic objects are treated as points, fields, densities, graphs, surfaces, or other geometric constructions.
Layer IV — Topology
The resulting structures are studied using persistent homology, persistence diagrams, persistence landscapes, persistence images, Wasserstein distances, bottleneck distances, and related topological tools.
Layer V — Astronomical Comparison
Arithmetic-derived structures are compared with independent astronomical data, including:
* galaxy distributions;
* clusters;
* filaments;
* voids;
* matter density;
* galaxy morphology;
* gravitational observables;
* radiation;
* redshift;
* galaxy environment;
* star formation rate;
* gas mass;
* stellar mass;
* other independently measured astronomical quantities.
Layer VI — Dynamics
Where static correspondence is supported, the program investigates whether arithmetic structures can describe or parameterize observed evolution across redshift or other observational variables.
Layer VII — Physical Theory
Only after empirical correspondence has been independently established may the program investigate whether an arithmetic structure can be incorporated into a physical theory involving:
* fields;
* gravity;
* thermodynamics;
* entropy;
* cohomology;
* scalar fields;
* variational principles;
* conservation laws;
* effective field theory;
* cosmological dynamics.
This ordering is mandatory.
A speculative physical interpretation must not be used as evidence for the empirical correspondence that motivated it.

3. Epistemic Classification System
Every substantive S.T.A.R. statement must be assigned an epistemic category.
The following categories are canonical.
Code
	Category
	Meaning
	D
	Derived
	Follows mathematically from stated assumptions or definitions
	E
	Empirical
	Directly supported by measured or computationally reproducible evidence
	M
	Model
	A mathematical or phenomenological construction introduced for investigation
	P
	Postulate
	An explicitly adopted assumption of a proposed theory
	H
	Hypothesis
	A proposition being experimentally tested
	C
	Conjecture
	A broader mathematical or physical proposition lacking proof
	T
	Testable Prediction
	A quantitative or qualitative consequence that can be tested independently
	S
	Speculative Interpretation
	A possible interpretation not yet supported sufficiently to be treated as a hypothesis or empirical conclusion
	These classifications are not interchangeable.
For example:
“Rank corresponds to cosmic complexity”
is a H.
The statement:
“The model predicts that rank distributions differ between clusters and voids”
is a T.
The statement:
“A measured dataset contains a statistically significant difference”
may be E, provided the analysis is reproducible and satisfies the statistical requirements of this Charter.
The statement:
“Therefore rank is the physical origin of cosmic structure”
remains S unless substantially stronger evidence and theory are established.

4.3 Candidate Mapping
A candidate mapping may take the form
[
\Phi:
\mathcal E
\rightarrow
\mathcal X,
]
where (\mathcal E) is a set of arithmetic objects and (\mathcal X) is a geometric representation space.
A historical candidate mapping is:
(\phi_E,\theta_E,z_E,s_E),
]
with quantities derived from arithmetic invariants.
A simplified historical form is:
\left(
\log|\Delta_E|,
\log N_E,
\alpha r_E
\right).
]
The mapping is a projection, not necessarily a bijection.
Different arithmetic objects may therefore map to equivalent or nearby geometric states.
No requirement of one-to-one correspondence shall be imposed unless an experiment specifically tests that property.

5. Candidate Mapping Families
S.T.A.R. retains multiple mapping families to prevent dependence on one historically selected construction.
These include:
5.1 (\Phi)
The primary historical arithmetic-invariant mapping family.
5.2 MCJ
A mapping involving the (j)-invariant or related modular information.
5.3 PTD
A predominantly algebraic mapping that minimizes dependence on analytic constructions.
5.4 FT
A Frobenius-trace-based mapping using finite-field information.
These alternative families are scientifically important because a correspondence that exists only under one specially selected mapping may be consistent with model-selection effects rather than an underlying physical relationship.

6. Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer Independence
The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture may provide historical or conceptual motivation for investigating elliptic-curve rank.
It is not a foundational assumption of S.T.A.R.
The research program must remain testable if BSD is removed from the theoretical motivation.
Consequently:
[
\text{BSD motivation}
\neq
\text{BSD dependence}.
]
A mapping or experiment that requires an unproven consequence of BSD must identify that dependency explicitly.
Where possible, the program shall use quantities that can be computed or independently estimated without assuming BSD.

10. Historical Numerical Thresholds
Historical numerical criteria must not automatically become scientific acceptance thresholds.
For example, a historical condition such as
[
W_2<10^{-2}
]
shall be classified as:
model-dependent / exploratory
until it has been recalibrated against appropriate null distributions, data resolution, noise, normalization, sample size, mapping alternatives, and parameter-selection effects.
Similarly, historical claims such as:
* (>98%) fidelity;
* (91.9%) correlation;
* (95.6%) correlation;
* fixed Wasserstein thresholds;
* symbolic-regression coefficients;
must be treated as results requiring validation rather than predetermined standards of success.
No threshold shall be considered canonical merely because it appeared in an earlier S.T.A.R. document.

12. Statistical Requirements
No empirical claim shall be accepted solely because a correlation coefficient, classifier accuracy, visual resemblance, or topological distance appears favorable.
Every substantive empirical claim must address, where applicable:
1. independent data;
2. sample definition;
3. selection effects;
4. null models;
5. randomization;
6. alternative mappings;
7. parameter selection;
8. train/validation/test separation;
9. out-of-sample performance;
10. multiple-hypothesis correction;
11. effect size;
12. uncertainty;
13. robustness to preprocessing;
14. robustness to resolution and scaling;
15. replication.
________________


13. Null Models
Null models are mandatory.
Appropriate nulls may include:
* randomized arithmetic assignments;
* randomized spatial assignments;
* permutation of rank values;
* shuffled invariant labels;
* synthetic point clouds;
* alternative astronomical catalog realizations;
* simulations without arithmetic information;
* alternative mapping families;
* mappings constructed from mathematically unrelated sequences.
The exact null must be matched to the hypothesis being tested.
A favorable result against an inappropriate null is not sufficient evidence.
________________


14. Alternative Mapping Controls
Whenever feasible, a proposed correspondence shall be tested against multiple mapping constructions.
For example:
[
\Phi,
\qquad
\Phi_{\rm MCJ},
\qquad
\Phi_{\rm PTD},
\qquad
\Phi_{\rm FT},
]
may be compared under identical evaluation procedures.
This determines whether an observed result is:
1. invariant across mapping families;
2. specific to one mapping;
3. sensitive to arbitrary parameter choices;
4. consistent with a broader arithmetic principle;
5. or potentially an artifact of the selected construction.
________________


15. Parameter Locking
Parameters used to construct a mapping must be distinguished from parameters discovered during validation.
Parameters selected after observing the target data are potentially subject to overfitting.
Accordingly, S.T.A.R. experiments should distinguish:
[
\text{pre-registered/fixed parameters}
]
from
[
\text{fitted/discovered parameters}.
]
A mapping that is optimized against the same dataset used to evaluate it cannot be treated as an independent prediction.
________________

Machine learning may be used for:
* feature discovery;
* nonlinear relationship discovery;
* classification;
* regression;
* dimensionality reduction;
* hypothesis generation.
Symbolic regression may be used to generate candidate mathematical relationships.
The canonical sequence is:
[
\text{ML discovery}
\rightarrow

\rightarrow
\text{independent validation}

\text{analytic derivation}.

Machine learning does not constitute mathematical proof.
A symbolic-regression expression is a candidate model until independently validated and, where applicable, mathematically derived.

Historical claims shall be reclassified when their original evidentiary basis does not satisfy the current Charter.

18.1 Historical correlations
A historical correlation is classified as E — preliminary empirical result only if its analysis can be reproduced and satisfies the applicable statistical requirements.
Otherwise it becomes:
[
\text{M/Historical Result}
]
or
[
\text{Exploratory}.
]
18.2 Historical thresholds
Thresholds such as:
[
W_2<10^{-2}

are automatically classified as:
model-dependent / exploratory
until independently recalibrated.
18.3 Historical symbolic laws
Expressions discovered through symbolic regression are classified as:
candidate models

18.4 Historical axioms
Statements previously presented as axioms that assert physical properties of the universe shall be reclassified as:
* hypotheses;

19. Statistical Significance Versus Predictive Utility
S.T.A.R. explicitly distinguishes statistical significance from practical predictive power.
A result may satisfy:
[
p < \alpha
]
and therefore provide evidence against a specified null hypothesis while still having negligible practical predictive utility.
Accordingly:
[
\text{statistical significance}
\neq
\text{predictive usefulness}.
]
A useful predictive model must demonstrate meaningful out-of-sample performance relative to appropriate baselines.
The Registry should therefore record both:
1. statistical evidence of correspondence; and
2. practical predictive utility.
A statistically significant but practically negligible relationship shall not be represented as a successful predictive theory.

28. Falsification
A valid S.T.A.R. hypothesis must admit the possibility of failure.
Examples include:
Mapping failure
Alternative mappings consistently outperform the proposed mapping or the proposed mapping fails against null models.
Rank failure
Rank contains no reproducible information about cosmic-web structure after controlling for relevant confounders.
Topological failure
Arithmetic persistence diagrams do not correspond to astronomical topology beyond null expectations.
Predictive failure
A statistically significant relationship has negligible out-of-sample predictive utility.
Replication failure
The relationship disappears in independent datasets.
Physical failure
A proposed arithmetic field conflicts with established gravitational, cosmological, laboratory, or astrophysical constraints.
Negative results shall be retained in the research archive.
A failed hypothesis is a valid scientific result.
________________


29. Success-Level Ladder
S.T.A.R. uses the following hierarchy.
Level 0 — Mathematical Construction
The mathematical object is precisely defined.
Level 1 — Computational Reproducibility
Independent execution reproduces the result.
Level 2 — Statistical Correspondence
The result demonstrates statistically supported correspondence against appropriate null models.
Level 3 — Cross-Dataset Replication
The correspondence survives independent datasets or independently constructed samples.
Level 4 — Predictive Correspondence
The construction predicts previously unseen data with meaningful out-of-sample utility.
Level 5 — Physical Theory
A mathematically consistent theory produces empirically successful physical predictions while satisfying known constraints.
Importantly:
[
\text{Level 2}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Level 4}.
]
Statistical significance alone does not establish practical predictive power.
Likewise:
[
\text{Level 4}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Level 5}.
]
Predictive performance does not by itself establish a fundamental physical theory.
________________

34. Governing Scientific Principle
The governing principle of S.T.A.R. is:
Do not ask whether the theory can be made to fit the universe. Ask whether a fixed mathematical construction predicts structure that was not used to construct it. 
                                             ~ Michael Dine ~
This principle controls the relationship between theory development and empirical testing.
A construction that is flexible enough to reproduce an already-used dataset does not thereby establish correspondence.
The scientific objective is instead:
[
\boxed{
\text{fixed construction}
\rightarrow
\text{independent prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{independent observation}
}
]
with explicit null models and reproducible analysis.

36. Controlling Standard for the Claim/Evidence Registry
The Claim/Evidence Registry shall use this Charter as its controlling standard.
For every claim, the Registry must answer five fundamental questions:
1. What exactly is being claimed?
The statement must be sufficiently precise to be tested.
2. What is the epistemic status?
The claim must be classified as:
[
D,\ E,\ M,\ P,\ H,\ C,\ T,\ \text{or }S.
]
3. What evidence supports it?
Evidence must be distinguished from assumptions, model outputs, historical observations, and interpretations.
4. What could falsify it?
A claim without a meaningful failure condition shall not be represented as a fully testable empirical hypothesis.
5. Has it survived independent validation?
A result derived from the same data or model used to construct the claim shall not automatically be treated as independent confirmation.
The Registry therefore functions as the scientific memory of S.T.A.R.
It shall preserve:
* positive results;
* negative results;
* unresolved claims;
* competing explanations;
* historical claims;
* superseded interpretations;
* model-dependent thresholds;
* failed experiments;
* successful replications;
* unresolved mathematical questions.
________________

No step may be silently skipped.
The purpose of this Charter is not to restrict the scope of S.T.A.R.'s ideas.
Its purpose is to ensure that increasingly ambitious mathematical and physical interpretations remain distinguishable from the evidence supporting them.
The project may ultimately discover:
* a genuine arithmetic–cosmic correspondence;
* a useful mathematical representation with no fundamental physical interpretation;
* an astrophysically useful predictive structure;
* a new mathematical relationship;
* or that particular proposed correspondences do not survive testing.
All of these outcomes are scientifically meaningful.
The controlling objective is therefore not confirmation of ACSC, GLMPCT, ECC, RTCH, rank-based cosmology, or any other predetermined theory.
The controlling objective is the discovery of whatever reproducible mathematical and physical relationships the evidence supports.
