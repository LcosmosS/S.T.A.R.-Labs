1. Introduction
The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture, a pivotal conjecture in number theory, relates an elliptic curve’s rank to its L-function’s behavior at s=1. 


Drawing inspiration from this, we propose the BSD Cosmological 3D Map Theory, defining a cosmological L-function:
L_{\text{cosmo}}(s) = \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \frac{M_0}{M_i} \right)^s
where M_i represents galaxy stellar masses, and M_0 is the median stellar mass. Our hypothesis asserts that for large samples with log-normally distributed stellar masses, the normalization constant:
K_{\text{theory}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (M_i / M_0)}{\sum_{i=1}^N (M_0 / M_i)}
approximates 1. 


This study validates this hypothesis using simulated and real data from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) DR17, employing PARI/GP for high-precision computations and Python for statistical analysis, aiming to develop a 3D cosmological map with galaxy distances via the Hubble-Lemaître law and density as topography.

2. Mathematical Foundation
Rationale for Log-Normal Distributions
Empirical studies indicate that galaxy stellar masses often follow log-normal distributions due to multiplicative growth processes in galaxy formation (Kauffmann et al., 2003). This distribution choice is logical, reflecting hierarchical merging dynamics, and aligns with our adaptation of the BSD conjecture’s normalization principle to a cosmological context.
Derivation of K_{\text{theory}}
For \log M_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), the median M_0 = e^\mu. Define X_i = \log M_i - \mu, so X_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2). Then:
K_{\text{theory}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N e^{X_i}}{\sum_{i=1}^N e^{-X_i}}
As ( N ) becomes large, the law of large numbers ensures:
* \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N e^{X_i} \to \mathbb{E}[e^{X_i}] = e^{\sigma^2 / 2}
* \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N e^{-X_i} \to \mathbb{E}[e^{-X_i}] = e^{\sigma^2 / 2}
Thus:
K_{\text{theory}} \approx \frac{N \cdot e^{\sigma^2 / 2}}{N \cdot e^{\sigma^2 / 2}} = 1
This derivation logically extends the BSD conjecture’s normalization to cosmology, predicting K_{\text{theory}} \approx 1 for large, log-normal samples—a testable hypothesis mirroring elliptic curve L-function properties.

3. Data Selection and Preparation


We sourced data from "Stellar_Mass2_Table.csv" (SDSS DR17), initially comprising 10,000 galaxy entries with stellar masses, SFRs, redshifts, and six additional parameters across nine columns. To ensure data reliability, we excluded entries with invalid values in any column, reducing the dataset to approximately 8,800 entries. This filtering, consistent with SDSS data processing standards, mitigated biases from incomplete records.


For subgroup analysis, we targeted low SFR galaxies within z = 0.05 to 0.1, defined by a standard SFR threshold (assumed conventional, e.g., log10(sSFR) < -11). Initial extraction yielded only 5 galaxies, significantly fewer than expected (hundreds), suggesting a filtering error addressed later.

4. Simulation Process


Choice of PARI/GP
PARI/GP was selected for its high-precision arithmetic, ideal for number-theoretic and statistical computations (PARI/GP Documentation, 2023). Lacking a native normal random number generator, we implemented the Box-Muller transform, a standard method converting uniform variates to normal ones (Box & Muller, 1958).






Simulation Design
We simulated log-mass data with N = 500, \mu = 9.7843, and \sigma = 0.3645, reflecting typical SDSS low SFR galaxy characteristics. For each simulation, K_{\text{theory}} was computed, and the process repeated 100 times across two clusters, yielding mean K_{\text{theory}} values close to 1.
